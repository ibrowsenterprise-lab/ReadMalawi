The Blue Rain Notebook

A free fictional story written for ReadMalawi · English

Every afternoon, Patrick and Lina argued about the weather. “It always rains on Tuesdays,” Patrick said. “No, Thursdays!” Lina replied.

Their science teacher did not choose a side. Instead, she gave them a blue notebook. “Write down what you observe for two weeks,” she said. “Then we can look for a pattern.”

Each school day the friends stood beneath the classroom veranda and watched the sky from a safe, dry place. They recorded the date, whether the ground was wet and whether they saw rain during their observation.

On one Tuesday, clouds covered the sky, but not a drop fell while they watched. On Thursday, a heavy shower passed over the school. Another week, both days stayed dry.

“I think our guesses were too big,” Patrick admitted as they reviewed the pages.

“A cloudy sky is not the same as rain,” Lina said. “And we only watched for a short time. It might rain when we are not looking.”

The teacher agreed. “Excellent observations. Your notebook tells us what you actually saw. It cannot tell us everything that happened all day.”

Lina drew a small chart, and Patrick wrote a heading above it: WHAT WE KNOW SO FAR.

After that, whenever they disagreed, they reached for the blue notebook before deciding who was right.
