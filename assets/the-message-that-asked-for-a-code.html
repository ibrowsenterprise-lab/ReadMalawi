The Message That Asked for a Code

A free fictional story written for ReadMalawi · English

Sella was helping her older brother practise reading on a phone when a message appeared. It said they had won a prize and must reply with a secret sign-in code before the prize expired.

“A prize!” her brother said. “But we did not enter a competition,” Sella replied.

A second message arrived. It was even more urgent. It told them not to tell anyone and promised to send the prize as soon as they shared the code.

Sella remembered something their teacher had said: a code sent to help you sign in belongs to you, not to a stranger asking for it.

She did not reply or tap the link. Instead, she showed the messages to their aunt. Their aunt helped them check the account through its usual app, not through the message.

“You were right to be careful,” her aunt said. “People can pretend to be trusted companies or friends. If you are unsure, stop and check with someone you trust.”

Sella's brother frowned. “So the urgent message was trying to rush us?”

“Exactly,” said their aunt. “A real decision can wait long enough for you to check it.”

Later, Sella wrote a reminder on a piece of paper: KEEP SIGN-IN CODES PRIVATE. STOP. CHECK. ASK.

Her brother read the words aloud. This time, both of them understood why they mattered.
