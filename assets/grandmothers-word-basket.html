Grandmother's Word Basket

A free fictional story written for ReadMalawi · English

On a quiet evening, Mwayi sat beside her grandmother while the family shelled groundnuts.

“Tell us the story about the clever hare,” Mwayi begged. Her grandmother laughed. “I learned that story from my own grandmother. She told it in a language we spoke at home.”

Mwayi understood most of the story, but a few words were unfamiliar. Instead of pretending, she stopped her grandmother and asked what they meant.

Her grandmother explained the words, then told the next part slowly. Mwayi retold it in English so her younger cousin could follow along.

“That is close,” her grandmother said, “but the hare was being playful, not cruel. Those are different things.”

Mwayi tried again, choosing words that kept the meaning. Her cousin smiled. “Now I understand why everyone laughed.”

The next day, Mwayi brought a notebook to visit her grandmother. At the top she wrote WORD BASKET. She left space for each word, what it meant and who had helped explain it.

“There are many languages in Malawi,” she said. “Could we collect stories from people who speak them?”

“Yes,” her grandmother replied. “But always listen carefully, ask permission and let speakers check your words before sharing them.”

Mwayi looked at the first page of her notebook. It was almost empty. That did not worry her. A good basket could be filled one careful word at a time.
