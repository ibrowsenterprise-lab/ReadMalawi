Tadala's Market Puzzle

A free fictional story written for ReadMalawi · English

Tadala loved visiting the market with her aunt on Saturday mornings. She liked the voices, the colours and the neat rows of fruit.

One morning, her aunt put three small baskets on the table. Each basket held four mangoes. “How many mangoes do we have?” she asked.

Tadala started counting each mango, but the baskets kept moving as customers looked at them. So she tried another way.

“Four, eight, twelve,” she said, pointing to each basket. “Three groups of four make twelve.”

Her aunt nodded. “Now suppose five mangoes are sold. How many remain?”

Tadala held up five fingers, then counted backwards from twelve: eleven, ten, nine, eight, seven. She checked by moving five mangoes to one side and counting the rest.

“Seven!” she said. “We have seven left.”

A customer overheard them. “You are quick with numbers,” she said. Tadala grinned. “Not always. I first had to find a way that worked.”

Before going home, Tadala wrote two lines in a little notebook: STARTED WITH 12. SOLD 5. LEFT 7.

“Numbers tell a story too,” her aunt said. “If we write down what happened, we can check it later.”

Tadala closed the notebook. The market had become her classroom for the day.
