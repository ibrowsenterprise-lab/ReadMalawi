# ReadMalawi — Physical Book Collection and School/Prison Handover Playbook

**Internal planning draft · 13 October 2026**  
**Status:** PRELAUNCH TEST ONLY — no pickups, collections, school or prison distributions have been completed or authorised through this system.

## Guiding rule

**Make giving easy for the donor; make accountability strong for the ReadMalawi team.**

A donor should only need to tell us roughly **how many physical books** they have and their **general town or area**. All additional arrangements happen in a private WhatsApp conversation *only when necessary*. No long website form, lists of titles or authors, full postal addresses or donor account creation.

ReadMalawi's existing book-sharing activity is the WhatsApp forum. Physical collections and deliveries to institutions are **planned future initiatives**, not historic achievements or guaranteed pickup services.

## Phase 1 — The book offer

1. Donor enters book count and general area on the public ReadMalawi page; the site opens a pre-filled WhatsApp message. **The donor chooses whether and where to send it.**
2. In test mode, the message clearly says the offer is fictional/prelaunch; no collection obligation is created.
3. Only after a coordinator later has permission to operate, they would thank the donor, confirm the approximate count and general area, and ask **whether** the donor wants collection or prefers an agreed drop-off. Avoid asking for a detailed home address in a public WhatsApp group.
4. A librarian may create a TEST lead internally, using the same two fields. Never store WhatsApp phone numbers, identity details, chat content, home addresses or photos in this test register.

## Phase 2 — Planning a possible collection (future, not active)

Before proposing a pickup:
- Confirm the programme is authorised to collect books, and that there is an accountable coordinator and at least one authorised collector.
- Check the team's available route and transport capacity, expected time, costs and safety conditions. Do not promise a pickup date or free transport until these are verified.
- In a **private** WhatsApp conversation, ask the donor's consent for any contact or pickup details. Share address and contact numbers only with the assigned collector; do not post them to the community group or public website.
- Agree an approximate pickup count; do not require title, ISBN or author lists from the donor.
- Do not ask for money, transport deposits or bank details in a book donation conversation.

## Phase 3 — Physical receipt and sorting (future, not active)

When a genuine authorised collection programme begins, introduce a **separate real inventory and receipt register**. The current TEST ledger must not serve as proof of receipt.

- Two responsible team members should count books together where practicable, acknowledge what was actually received and reconcile differences from the offered count.
- Identify obvious issues such as heavy mould, contamination, torn or unreadable materials, missing pages, extreme wear or age-inappropriate material. Politely decline unsafe items.
- Sort by broad reading level, language and genre **internally** after collection. Donors are not required to classify their books.
- Use an aggregate internal receipt showing date, count received, broad category and collector acknowledgements. Do not publicly publish donor identity, a private address or child-related details.
- Protect books against rain, loss, theft and damage in transport/storage. No publication of collection figures until they have actually been verified.

## Phase 4 — Selecting recipient institutions (future, not active)

- For schools, obtain prior agreement with school leadership, assess language/age appropriateness and coordinate a realistic handover.
- For prisons, contact the responsible prison authority first. Obtain explicit institutional approval, discuss safety/content and admission controls, delivery schedules and whether specific items are permitted. Never assume an institution can receive books simply because a donor offered them.
- Match suitable volumes to each receiving institution's agreed requirements. Keep a separate count of books assigned, dispatched and acknowledged on receipt; totals must reconcile.
- Obtain a responsible staff member's acknowledgement of handover. Any photographs, prisoners' details or learners' identities require proper authorisation and safeguarding review; default to aggregate reporting.

## Phase 5 — Honest community impact reporting (future, not active)

Use **separate** metrics, never merge them:
- **Offered:** approximate books proposed by community members; not received.
- **Collected:** physical copies actually counted and received.
- **Distributed:** books handed to a named, authorised institution and acknowledged by the recipient.
- **Beneficiaries:** only numbers grounded in institutional evidence; never infer the number of readers from the number of books.

Report where relevant by date, region and institution type; avoid reporting identifiable schoolchildren, prisoners or donors. The current public website should continue to say that physical distribution is only a future objective.

## Safe donor reply templates (not automatically sent)

**Acknowledging interest during testing:**  
“Thank you for thinking of ReadMalawi! We are preparing a future physical-book collection programme. Your offer is appreciated. No pickup is scheduled at this stage. We will share verified collection arrangements when the programme is authorised.”

**Once the programme is officially authorised (not for use yet):**  
“Thank you for offering approximately [count] books in [general area]. Would you prefer to discuss a possible collection or an agreed drop-off? We will confirm availability with the collection team first. Please share any private pickup details only in this one-to-one chat, not in the group.”

**A prospective school/prison partner (not for use yet):**  
“ReadMalawi is planning an educational book initiative and would welcome a preliminary discussion about your institution's needs and permission requirements. We have not scheduled any donation. Please advise whom our coordinator should contact through your official channel.”

## Prelaunch approval checklist

- [ ] Founder explicitly approves launching a real collection programme, including its legal/operating status and approved public wording.
- [ ] Responsible coordinators and collection volunteers are assigned and trained.
- [ ] Transport, storage, collection safety and operating funds are agreed.
- [ ] Donor consent, data minimisation, incident reporting and retention rules approved.
- [ ] Book receipt, sorting and verified handover registers designed **separately** from TEST enquiries.
- [ ] Schools and prisons give the required permissions before any physical distribution.
- [ ] No donor cash payments, unofficial receipts or unverified charitable status claimed.
- [ ] Public website copy reviewed and a founder-approved release explicitly distinguishes the now-active programme from the earlier prelaunch.

**Do not switch ReadMalawi's `sitePhase` or remove TEST-only database constraints simply to start collecting. A separate authorised launch and live operational register are necessary.**
