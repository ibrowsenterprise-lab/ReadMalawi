Short story · Fiction

The Book on the Bench

On her way home, Chisomo noticed a book lying on a bench near the market. Inside the front cover was a message: “Please read me, then pass me on.”

She opened the book and found a story about a young girl who loved solving difficult problems. Chisomo read a few pages while waiting for her friend. Soon, she was so interested that she forgot about the noisy market around her.

That evening, Chisomo told her younger brother about the story. He asked whether he could read it too. They agreed to take turns, then return the book to the bench with a new note inviting another reader to enjoy it.

Question: Why did Chisomo and her brother agree to return the book?

Why start this way?

Members can try AI-assisted learning immediately, while ReadMalawi measures interest and learns what readers actually need. There is no need to pay for an API before the community is active.

  • Works on most phones with a browser and data connection.
  • No member accounts or personal data collection on this starter site.
  • Human moderators can choose weekly themes and moderate group discussions.
  • A secure, dedicated AI assistant can be considered after testing demand and budgeting for it.

AI tools can make mistakes, especially with local-language nuance. Important explanations and translations should be checked by people fluent in the language.

Our history, told accurately

A vision from 2019. A new digital chapter.

Founded by Jones Nalikungwi

ReadMalawi began as a vision to expand access to books in Malawi. In 2019, The Nation reported on the proposed initiative, including earlier discussions with the late Malawian writer and historian DD Phiri.

Its activity to date has been digital book sharing through a WhatsApp community. Physical book distribution to schools and prisons remains a future ambition, not a claimed past achievement.

Independent community initiative · Not yet a registered NGO

ReadMalawi in the news

“DD Phiri reading project in offing” — The Nation, 12 June 2019 (archived copy).

We remember DD Phiri with respect. His historical discussions about the proposal must not be understood as an endorsement of any current programme.

Frequently asked questions

Clear from the beginning.

Is ReadMalawi already a registered NGO?

No. It is currently a digital book-sharing community with plans for a future physical-book donation programme, subject to the required registration or authorised partnerships.

Can I donate money here?

No. This starter website does not collect funds, process payments or claim charitable registration.

Does ReadMalawi provide free copies of every book?

No. It points readers to authorised sources. Specific titles may have different copyright or licence restrictions.

Are all seven languages fully translated on the website?

Not yet. The language selector prepares prompts in your chosen language, and the only local-language story sample currently included is a Chichewa draft. Fluent reviewers are needed before full site translations are marked ready.

Does this website run its own AI?

No. It provides example prompts for use with an external AI service. A dedicated secure AI assistant is a possible later phase.
