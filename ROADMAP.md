# ReadMalawi — 90-day digital launch and sustainability plan

## Starting reality
Founder: Jones Nalikungwi. ReadMalawi was described publicly in 2019, and the founder reports 126 WhatsApp members. Digital book sharing is the only activity completed so far. The group is currently quiet. No verified physical book distributions, formal NGO registration or sponsor funds should be claimed.

## Weeks 1–2 | Restart with a useful service
- Publish the no-payments, digital-first reading site.
- Send a WhatsApp reintroduction and member-interest poll (education level, preferred language, reading interests; do not collect sensitive data).
- Run one easy reading challenge a week, with one group discussion.
- Recruit 2 moderators plus 1 English/Chichewa translation reviewer.

## Weeks 3–6 | Improve usefulness
- Offer the same original practice story/quiz in Chichewa only after human review.
- Record voluntary participation metrics: weekly active contributors, reading-challenge completions, feedback count, preferred languages, reviewer capacity. Publish only anonymous aggregates.
- Bring in volunteer reviewers for Chitumbuka, Chiyao, Chilomwe, Chisena and Chitonga.
- Contact libraries, educators and publishers for content permissions and collaboration.

## Weeks 7–12 | Establish credible sponsor material
- Compile a short impact report with actual activities (not assumed reach from total WhatsApp members).
- Invite an organisation to sponsor one named outcome: e.g. creation and review of ten original short reading lessons in two languages, or a reading workshop.
- Request in-kind support first (translation review, educational materials, internet bundles, professional mentors).
- Explore a fiscal host / authorised partner for any charitable grant or public fundraising.

## Possible later revenue routes (separate from free literacy access)
1. **Corporate-funded literacy programmes** with sponsor acknowledgement and audited delivery reports.
2. **Paid institutional workshops** on safe AI-assisted reading, prompt literacy, and language-localisation practice, run through a legally eligible entity.
3. **Original curriculum / story pack development** commissioned by schools, publishers or education partners; agree IP, editorial standards and licensing in writing.
4. **Qualified translation/editing services** for clients, using human reviewer approval and transparent AI assistance.
5. **Grants** for digital inclusion and local language literacy, via a registered eligible applicant or approved fiscal host.

Keep free public-domain/open-licensed reading access free. Never resell third-party copyrighted ebooks without permission, and check Creative Commons NonCommercial clauses before monetisation.

## Costs and controls
- GitHub Pages hosting: potentially MK0 on a public repository, not counting mobile data or domain costs.
- No paid AI API needed for version 1; readers use copyable prompts in their own AI accounts (subject to third-party terms and costs).
- Use volunteers only with clear scope, credit and review practices; track any expenses explicitly.
- Keep sponsor/grant income in the appropriate named entity's books, distinct from IBROWS Enterprise. Do not collect NGO donations as an unregistered body.
- If paid training or other commercial services become the main purpose, move transactional functions away from GitHub Pages to compliant hosting.

## Indicators to track
- Weekly contributors (not just 126 total)
- Clicks or quiz submissions only if collected lawfully and transparently; existing quiz progress is browser-local, not centrally reported
- Number of *human-reviewed* original stories/translations published
- Number of written partner commitments
- Actual sponsor value and spending only once authorised and recorded
